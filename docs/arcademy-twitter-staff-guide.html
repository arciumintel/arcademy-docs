Twitter Guide for Arcademy

This guide explains what Arcademy is, how the website works, and how to grow our Twitter presence — written in plain language.

Your job in one sentence: Help people understand that Arcademy turns confusing product docs into a clear, step-by-step path — and that finishing a program actually means they understood it, not just clicked through.

Last updated: May 2026 · Questions? Ask the team before posting anything you're unsure about.

The Pitch (Plain English)

Arcademy is a website that helps people get ready to use products in the Arcium ecosystem. Think of it like a friendly onboarding guide — not a school, not a course marketplace, and not a hype machine.

Today, when someone wants to learn a new crypto or developer product, they usually get a long, scattered documentation page. They scroll, get lost, and never know if they actually understood anything. Arcademy fixes that by offering guided programs: read a lesson, take a short check at the end, move to the next step. Progress is saved. Completion means something.

Memorize this line: Arcademy is onboarding infrastructure — guided programs with comprehension checks and measurable progress for the Arcium ecosystem.

The problem we solve

  • Docs are hard to follow when you're new.
  • People drop off and never come back.
  • Teams can't tell who actually understood the product vs. who just skimmed.
  • Support gets flooded with basic questions that good onboarding would prevent.

What we offer instead

  • One place (the hub) to discover onboarding programs.
  • Clear lesson-by-lesson paths with short quizzes to confirm understanding.
  • One account so users can pick up where they left off.
  • For partner teams: a governed way to turn their docs into structured programs without building their own system.

Launch focus

The first program live on Arcademy is Arcium Fundamentals — the starting path for anyone new to the Arcium ecosystem. Many early tweets can point people there.

What Arcademy Is Not

Getting this wrong on Twitter hurts trust. Avoid these frames:

Don't say Why Say instead
"Online school" / "LMS" / "take our courses" We're infrastructure for onboarding, not a generic education platform. "Guided onboarding programs" or "structured path to get started"
"Earn XP" / "level up" / "streaks" / "leaderboards" We deliberately avoid gamification theater. "Track your progress" / "pick up where you left off"
"Get rewarded with tokens" / "NFT badge" Not our model at launch. Future credentials are about proof of completion, not hype. "Verified completion" / "you finished the fundamentals"
"Revolutionary AI platform" Overpromising and buzzwords hurt our calm, credible brand. Specific, honest benefits (clarity, progress, readiness)
"Classroom" / "instructor" / "students enroll in classes" Wrong mental model. "Program" / "lesson" / "enroll in a program"

Simple Glossary

Use these words consistently in tweets and replies:

Word What it means (simple)
Hub The Arcademy home page — where people browse and discover programs.
Program A full onboarding path for one product (e.g. Arcium Fundamentals). This is what people "join."
Lesson One reading page in a program. Usually ends with a short quiz.
Track A group of lessons — like a chapter in a book.
Quiz A short comprehension check — not a scary exam. Confirms they got the key ideas.
Enroll When a user officially joins a program so their progress is tracked.
Partner An ecosystem team (a product or protocol) that uses Arcademy to onboard their users.
Arcium The ecosystem Arcademy serves first. Arcium Fundamentals is program #1.

Website Walkthrough

You don't need to explain code or backend systems. You do need to know what a visitor sees and what story to tell. Here's the site in order:

1. Home — the Hub (/)

The front door. Featured programs, a catalog feel, and (for signed-in users) "continue where you left off."

Tweet angle: "One home for getting started in the ecosystem — no doc maze."

2. Programs catalog (/programs)

Full list of available onboarding programs. Clean browsing — not a chaotic link dump.

Tweet angle: "Browse structured paths, not scattered wiki pages."

3. Program page (/programs/[name])

Overview of one program: what it covers, who it's for, and a button to enroll or continue. This is where someone commits to the path.

Tweet angle: "See the full path before you start — then enroll in one click."

4. Lesson reader (/programs/.../lessons/...)

The core experience: readable content (headings, paragraphs, callouts, images) plus a quiz at the end. Calm, focused, no distractions — like good documentation should feel.

Tweet angle: "Read → check your understanding → next lesson. Simple."

5. Account (/account)

Shows enrollments and progress across programs. One login, everything in one place.

Tweet angle: "Your progress follows you — come back anytime."

User journey (for thread tweets)

Land on hub → Pick a program → Try lesson 1 (guest OK) → Sign up to save → Complete lessons + quizzes → Ready to go deeper
Guest try: New visitors can try the first lesson without an account. Signing up saves their progress. That's a great hook for tweets — low friction, no commitment upfront.

Who We're Talking To on Twitter

Primary: New users & developers

  • Curious about Arcium but overwhelmed by docs.
  • Developers who want to build or integrate but need fundamentals first.
  • People who tried before, got lost, and bounced.

What they care about: Clarity, saving progress, not wasting time, feeling ready.

Secondary: Partner teams

  • Ecosystem products that need better onboarding.
  • Teams tired of support tickets for basic questions.
  • Leads who want measurable activation, not vanity metrics.

What they care about: Drop-off data, standardized paths, quality control.

Early Twitter growth will mostly come from the first group. Partner-facing posts can come later or live on a separate account if the team decides.

Brand Voice on Twitter

Arcademy should feel calm, credible, and educational — like a helpful guide, not a hype account.

Do sound like

  • Clear and specific
  • Warm but professional
  • Confident without shouting
  • Helpful — you're solving a real problem

Don't sound like

  • "🚀🚀🚀 LFG TO THE MOON"
  • Vague AI buzzwords
  • Condescending "you're doing it wrong"
  • Fake urgency ("LAST CHANCE!!!")

Formatting tips

  • Short sentences. One idea per tweet when possible.
  • Use line breaks for readability (especially in threads).
  • One link per tweet — usually to the hub or a program page.
  • Emojis: sparingly. Zero is fine. One relevant emoji max if it fits the tone.
  • Threads beat walls of text. 3–7 tweets is a sweet spot for explainers.

Twitter Growth Strategy

Growing a profile is a marathon. These tactics work for a product like Arcademy — educational, niche, trust-based.

Foundation (week 1–2)

  1. Profile setup: Clear bio (what Arcademy is + link to site). Same name everywhere: Arcademy. Banner: clean, readable, on-brand — ask design for assets.
  2. Pinned tweet: Best explainer thread or a single tweet with the clearest value prop + link.
  3. Pin a second tweet (if using Notes/long-form): FAQ or "Start here: Arcium Fundamentals."

Content mix (ongoing)

Type Share of posts Purpose
Educational / problem-aware ~40% Build trust — talk about onboarding pain without always selling
Product & feature highlights ~25% Show what Arcademy does with concrete examples
Social proof & milestones ~15% Launches, completions, partner news (when approved)
Engagement (replies, quotes, polls) ~15% Be visible in ecosystem conversations
Building in public / behind the scenes ~5% Humanize the brand — only what leadership approves

Growth tactics that actually work

  • Reply thoughtfully on Arcium ecosystem posts — add value, don't spam links. Link only when directly relevant.
  • Quote-tweet with context when partners or ecosystem accounts share news. Add "here's how to get started" when appropriate.
  • Threads weekly — one solid explainer beats ten weak one-liners.
  • Consistency > virality — 1–2 quality posts/day beats random bursts.
  • Cross-post carefully — Discord/Telegram announcements can become tweet threads with different hooks.
  • Track what works — note which tweets get saves, profile clicks, and link clicks. Do more of that format.
  • Collaborate — ask partners to quote or co-tweet launch posts (with approval).
  • Lists — follow and organize ecosystem accounts, dev influencers, and partner leads into private lists for daily engagement.

Metrics that matter

  • Profile visits and link clicks (did we drive people to the site?)
  • Saves and bookmarks (is the content useful enough to keep?)
  • Reply quality (are real people engaging?)
  • Follower growth — secondary. A smaller engaged audience beats a large dead one.
Never buy followers or use engagement pods. They destroy credibility and don't convert to real users.

Do's & Don'ts

Do

  • Link to the live site when inviting people to try Arcademy
  • Use "program," "lesson," "hub," "enroll"
  • Ask the team before announcing dates, partners, or features
  • Screenshot real product UI when you have assets
  • Credit partners when sharing their news
  • Save strong tweets as templates for reuse
  • Report confusing feedback to the team — it's content gold

Don't

  • Promise token rewards, NFTs, or "earn crypto for learning"
  • Call Arcademy a school, LMS, or MOOC
  • Attack competitors or other onboarding tools
  • Share internal screenshots with sensitive data
  • Engage in drama — stay helpful and neutral
  • Post the same link 10 times a day (spam filters hurt reach)
  • Invent features that aren't live yet

When to get approval first

  • Any post mentioning a partner by name
  • Launch dates, metrics, or user counts
  • Anything about pricing, legal, or data sharing
  • Crisis responses (outages, bugs, negative press)
  • Giveaways, contests, or paid promotions
